## 1. The decision you must make before you walk in

navAjna is hiring for **two separate tracks**, four openings each:

| Track | Primary stack | What Round 1 tests |
|---|---|---|
| **Backend Developer** | Java, Spring Boot, Microservices | Core Java **+ Spring Boot** |
| **Frontend Developer** | React.js, JavaScript / TypeScript | Core Java **+ React** |

**Both rounds happen on the same day.** Round 1 is technical — core Java plus your position's
skills. Round 2 is technical/managerial — project experience, problem solving and fitment.

### 🔴 My recommendation: go in for the **Frontend** track

This is the first role in your search where being full-stack is a straight advantage, and the
frontend track is where you clear the bar by the widest margin.

**Why frontend:**

- **The frontend JD's primary skills are your deepest work.** React + Redux, hooks and state
  management, ES6+/TypeScript, REST integration with JWT, Jest, browser debugging. You have
  every one of those in production, not in a tutorial.
- **It asks only for "working knowledge of Java"** as the secondary skill — and you have a
  year of production Spring Boot, JPA and Camunda. **You are not just at that bar, you are
  well over it.** That is a very comfortable place to be.
- **Your evidence is unusually concrete**: a 14-screen onboarding flow, the My Requests page,
  Lighthouse 62 → 88 with the actual mechanism, a React 18 / Next.js / TypeScript migration,
  reusable components reused across 3+ features, Jest and React Testing Library.

**Why not backend, even though it is real:** on the backend track you would be measured
against people whose *only* work is Spring Boot, and the JD adds **Oracle** and **Linux**,
which are your two thinnest areas. On the frontend track your Java becomes a bonus rather
than the thing being examined.

**But this is your call, and the backend case is legitimate** — you own two end-to-end Spring
Boot applications with Camunda workflows and an AOP audit system. If backend is where you
want your career to go, say so and interview for it. Just do not arrive undecided.

> **What to say at the desk when they ask which position:**
> *"Frontend primarily — React, Redux and TypeScript are my day-to-day. But I have also built
> the Spring Boot and JPA backends for the same applications, so I can work across both if
> that is useful to you."*
>
> That sentence puts you in the stronger queue while telling them you can fill either gap.
> With four openings on each side, that flexibility is worth real money to them.

The JD says it twice, in bold terms: **"must be able to join within 0–7 days. Candidates with
longer notice periods will not be considered."**

Most candidates in a walk-in queue are serving 30, 60 or 90 days. **You can start next week.**

— it may be the deciding one.

**Say it in your first two minutes**, in the "tell me about yourself", not at the end:
> "…and I am available to join immediately, so the 7-day window is not an issue for me."

interviewer will notice and it needs a prepared, calm, two-sentence answer. **File 04, §2.**

## 3. What navAjna actually is, and why it matters

A Hyderabad product-and-services firm doing **software modernisation and product innovation**.
The JD is explicit that you **work across multiple client projects over time** — different
domains, codebases and stacks.

**Three things that tells you:**

1. **They value adaptability over narrow depth.** Someone who has worked both ends of the
   stack and migrated a platform to React 18 / Next.js / TypeScript fits that story exactly.
   Say it.
2. **"Interest in learning new technologies" is in the JD.** Have a real answer — what you
   have learned since February, and how you learn.
3. **It is a smaller company than Infosys or a bank.** The people interviewing you may well
   be the people you would work with. That makes Round 2 — "fitment" — genuinely about
   whether they want you on the team. Be someone they would enjoy sitting next to.

## 4. How to use this sheet

It is deliberately short. You already have the long guides; this is the walk-in version.

**Every technical topic also explains where the memory goes**, in plain English with a small
example — stack versus heap, what an object actually costs, why mutating state does nothing in
React, why a forgotten timer is a memory leak. Memory is asked directly in Java interviews, and
knowing where things live is what makes the other answers make sense rather than be memorised.
**File 01, Part 0 is the foundation — read that first.**

| Section | Read when |
|---|---|
| `01-core-java.md` | **Everyone reads this.** Round 1 opens here regardless of track. |
| `02-backend-track.md` | If you go backend — or to be safe, skim it anyway |
| `03-frontend-track.md` | **If you take my recommendation, this is your main file** |
| `04-round2-and-you.md` | Your project stories, the gap, why navAjna, questions to ask |
| `05-walk-in-checklist.md` | The night before and the morning of |

**Priority if you are short of time:** `01` → `03` (or `02`) → `04`. Section 04 matters more
than people expect, because Round 2 is explicitly about fitment.
